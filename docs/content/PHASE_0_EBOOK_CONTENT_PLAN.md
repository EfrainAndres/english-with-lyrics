# Phase 0 Ebook Content Plan

## Overview

This document defines the strategic, editorial, educational, legal-safe, and design-ready content plan for the first free ebook produced by Sing Pronunce Repeat / English with Lyrics. It covers purpose, positioning, target reader, benchmark analysis, scope, structure, song-selection framework, micro-lesson template, editorial rules, legal rules, exercise library, production workflow, quality gates, and open decisions.

This document does not contain the ebook itself, song lyrics, a PDF, or final song selections.

---

## 1. Purpose and Validation Objective

The ebook is a Phase 0.5 lead magnet. Its role is to generate qualified email interest and provide early evidence that people want to learn English through songs before the paid MVP is built.

### What the ebook is designed to validate

- Whether Spanish speakers want structured, song-based English practice.
- Whether they respond to the Escríbelo como suena method as a pronunciation bridge.
- Which musical categories create the strongest engagement (romantic, rock/pop classic, beginner-friendly).
- Whether learners are interested in pronunciation guidance, listening confidence, vocabulary, and practical phrases.
- Whether ebook readers convert to survey respondents and first-group candidates.
- Whether this audience will prepare to pay for a structured 5-song, 25-micro-lesson MVP.

### How it fits the funnel

The ebook is not the product. It is the first real artifact of the product experience. It leads users toward:

1. The validation survey: `https://tally.so/r/eqzgbe`
2. The first-group interest form: `https://tally.so/r/D4a6NE`
3. The download page: `/ebook-gratis`
4. The channel: English with Lyrics

### Important caveat

Ebook downloads alone do not prove willingness to pay. A learner who downloads a free guide has not validated demand for the MVP. High download volume with low survey completion and low first-group interest is a medium signal, not a strong one. The ebook must function as a conversion tool toward high-intent signals, not as an endpoint.

## 3. Target Reader

### Primary reader

- Spanish-speaking learner at beginner or intermediate level.
- Interested in music as a learning tool.
- Has difficulty understanding English as it sounds in songs.
- Feels insecure about pronunciation.
- Wants practical, accessible guidance without IPA symbols.
- May arrive from YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, or the Sing Pronunce Repeat landing page.
- Primarily reads from a phone.
- Prefers short, clear formats over long courses or dense grammar books.
- Motivated by real songs they know or want to understand.
- Wants to feel capable and confident, not graded or corrected.

### Who the ebook is not primarily designed for

- Advanced phonetics specialists seeking rigorous transcription.
- Teachers looking for a complete classroom curriculum or lesson plan bank.
- Learners looking for full song lyrics or official translations.
- Learners looking for a karaoke or sing-along replacement.
- Learners who already have high listening confidence in English.

## 4. Benchmark and Reference Analysis

### Purpose

This section reviews existing song-based language learning content to identify structural patterns this ebook should adopt, adapt, or avoid. Named references are used only as structural category examples. No content, layout, exercise design, or branding from any reference has been copied. Claims about specific catalog sizes, pricing, or traffic are not made because they cannot be verified from project documentation.

### Benchmark table

| Reference or resource type | Typical format | Useful pattern | Weakness or risk | What this project should adopt conceptually | What this project must do differently |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESL song worksheets (e.g., iSLCollective, ESLSongs-style) | PDF or printable worksheet; gap-fill exercises with lyrics | Short focused practice; one song per worksheet; clear activity flow | Often depend on full lyrics or large lyric blocks; teacher-led; not mobile-friendly | Short practice blocks per song; clear activity structure; one goal per page | Must not use gap-fill of complete or near-complete lyrics; must be self-guided; must be readable on phone |
| ESOL Courses song-based activities | Structured activity with listening, vocabulary, and reflection tasks | Combining listening, vocabulary, and meaning in one activity | Often use longer fragments; classroom-oriented; not pronunciation-first | Integrating listening + meaning + vocabulary in one short flow | Must prioritize pronunciation and use Escríbelo como suena; must serve independent learners |
| Teaching English with Songs-style materials | Teacher-led lesson plan with song intro, listening task, discussion, and grammar focus | Warm-up listening; step-by-step flow; reflection questions | Designed for teacher-led classrooms; grammar-heavy; not useful alone | Progressive flow: listen → read → understand → practice | Must remove teacher dependency; must not be grammar-heavy; must be phone-first |
| English-with-songs workbooks (general) | Longer workbook or multi-chapter guide; may include dozens of songs | Depth of vocabulary and cultural context | Often too long; too much content per song; not beginner-friendly | Choosing depth over breadth; one song well-taught instead of many songs shallow | Must keep scope to 3 songs and 8–14 pages; must feel achievable in one sitting |
| Pronunciation guides for language learners | Phonetic charts, IPA guides, minimal pairs; sometimes MP3 audio | Systematic pronunciation focus; clear visual structure | Usually too abstract or technical; IPA is not beginner-friendly | Systematic structure; one pronunciation pattern per section | Must use Escríbelo como suena, not IPA; must use song context, not abstract drills |
| Educational PDF lead magnets (general) | Short 5–20 page PDF; one topic; CTA at the end | Short, scannable; one clear takeaway; strong CTA | Sometimes low quality; generic; no educational depth | Scannable structure; clear takeaway per section; one primary CTA | Must add real teaching value, not just collect leads; must be genuinely useful without the CTA conversion |
| Short mobile-first learning guides | Cards or scroll-friendly layouts; short text blocks; progress markers | Mobile readability; short blocks; progress checkboxes | Sometimes shallow; no skill development | Card-style blocks; short paragraphs; progress checkboxes | Must maintain educational depth alongside mobile readability |
| Music-based lesson resources for teachers (YouTube-adjacent) | Video lesson companion, worksheet, or lesson summary | Rich cultural context; high learner motivation | Usually companion to a video; does not stand alone | High motivation framing; cultural context about the song | Must stand alone without requiring a companion video; must link to official sources instead |

### Benchmark conclusions

**Lyric dependence.** Many existing song resources depend heavily on lyric gap-fill exercises. This ebook must not reproduce complete lyrics or become a lyric-completion worksheet. Short educational fragments with original explanation are the only legally and editorially safe path.

**Teacher orientation.** Many existing materials are teacher-led and classroom-oriented. This ebook must be self-guided and friendly for independent learners who do not have an instructor.

**Grammar vs. pronunciation.** Many resources prioritize grammar and isolated vocabulary. This ebook should prioritize pronunciation, listening confidence, useful phrases, and natural Spanish meaning.

**Visual density.** Many worksheets are visually dense. This ebook must be easy to read on a phone with short blocks, adequate spacing, and clear typography hierarchy.

**Spanish-speaker gap.** Most existing resources are not designed specifically for Spanish speakers. Escríbelo como suena is the central differentiator that no commonly observed worksheet or workbook provides.

**Feel.** The result should feel like guided musical practice, not homework.

### Ideas we can adapt without copying

- **Short learning blocks.** One short fragment per block with a clear goal.
- **Repetition prompts.** Explicit instructions to repeat a phrase two or three times.
- **Progress checkboxes.** A small checkbox at the end of each section so the reader feels completion.
- **Mini challenges.** A brief two- or three-step challenge at the end of the guide.
- **Visual vocabulary cards.** A card-style block that isolates one or two key words with meaning and a usage example.
- **Listen–read–repeat flow.** A clear instructional sequence the reader follows for each phrase.
- **Practical use examples.** An original sentence showing how a phrase works in a real context outside of the song.
- **A final action plan.** A short summary of what was practiced and a clear next step.

### Patterns to avoid

- Full lyric reproduction in any form.
- Long gap-fill activities that require the learner to read or reconstruct a verse.
- Dense classroom worksheets designed for a teacher to manage.
- Excessive grammar explanations unconnected to the listening goal.
- Generic or inconsistent pronunciation spelling that changes conventions across pages.
- Too many songs or exercises that make the guide feel like a course.
- Forced registration steps inside the PDF (the CTA should lead to an external form, not block the reader).
- Layouts that resemble or copy identifiable competitor materials.
- Unsupported claims such as "learn English fluently with 3 songs."

## 5. Recommended Ebook Scope

### Core recommendation

- **3 songs.** One guide covering three songs keeps the scope achievable, the production time low, and the educational focus clear.
- **3 short educational phrases per song** as the initial baseline. Each phrase must be short enough to teach in one focused block.
- **Maximum 5 phrases per song** only if page count and legal review support it. Do not exceed this in the first version.
- **8 to 14 pages.** This range is achievable on a phone in one sitting and keeps PDF production manageable.
- **Mobile-readable layout.** Assume the reader will view the PDF on a phone, not a desktop.
- **Short completion time.** The guide should be completable in 20–40 minutes of focused reading and practice.
- **One clear learning outcome per section.** Each page or block should deliver one thing the reader can do or understand by the end.

### Why small is better for Phase 0 validation

A larger ebook does not produce better validation evidence. A 3-song, 8–14 page guide:

- Can be produced and reviewed faster than a 10-song guide.
- Tests whether learners engage with the format before committing to a longer version.
- Is less likely to have legal or editorial issues that require revisions.
- Delivers a quick win that motivates survey completion and first-group interest.
- Can be expanded in a future version if demand is confirmed.

Producing a comprehensive ebook before demand is validated is a Phase 1 decision, not a Phase 0 decision.

## 6. Proposed Ebook Structure

### Section-by-section outline

#### Cover

- **Purpose:** First impression. Brand identification. Motivation to open the guide.
- **Approximate length:** 1 page.
- **Main content:** Title, subtitle, brand name (Sing Pronunce Repeat / English with Lyrics), method name (Escríbelo como suena), and brand palette.
- **User action:** Open the guide.
- **Validation value:** None direct, but design quality affects perceived credibility.

#### Welcome

- **Purpose:** Set the context. Tell the reader what they will practice and why it works.
- **Approximate length:** 1 short page or half page.
- **Main content:** A brief warm greeting, what the reader will gain from the guide, and how the guide is organized. Use the brand voice: warm, encouraging, practical.
- **User action:** Read and feel motivated to continue.
- **Validation value:** Low direct. Sets the tone for engagement.

#### What Escríbelo como suena is

- **Purpose:** Introduce the method before the reader encounters it in practice.
- **Approximate length:** Half page or short card block.
- **Main content:** A plain-language explanation of the method. It writes English pronunciation in Spanish-friendly spelling so the reader can approximate sounds without IPA. It is a bridge, not a perfect transcription. It encourages listening to official audio.
- **User action:** Understand the method before practicing it.
- **Validation value:** Medium. If learners mention this section in feedback, it signals that the differentiator is landing.

#### How to use the guide

- **Purpose:** Remove friction. Tell the reader exactly how to move through the guide.
- **Approximate length:** Half page.
- **Main content:** A short numbered sequence. For example: listen to the song first, then work through each phrase block, then try the practice prompts, then complete the three-day challenge at the end.
- **User action:** Understand the expected flow before starting.
- **Validation value:** Low direct. Reduces drop-off caused by confusion about format.

#### Song 1 guided micro-lesson

- **Purpose:** Deliver the first educational experience.
- **Approximate length:** 2–3 pages.
- **Main content:** Song title, artist, learner level, why this song works, link to official audio, three short phrase blocks (each with Escríbelo como suena, natural meaning, vocabulary note, and a practice prompt), one mini exercise, and a progress checkbox.
- **User action:** Listen, read, repeat, and mark complete.
- **Validation value:** High. Feedback about Song 1 reveals which content format and which song type engage learners most.

#### Song 2 guided micro-lesson

- **Purpose:** Extend the experience with a different category or mood.
- **Approximate length:** 2–3 pages.
- **Main content:** Same structure as Song 1 with a different song, category, and phrase set.
- **User action:** Listen, read, repeat, and mark complete.
- **Validation value:** High. Comparing engagement across Song 1 and Song 2 informs the final song selection strategy.

#### Song 3 guided micro-lesson

- **Purpose:** Complete the three-song arc.
- **Approximate length:** 2–3 pages.
- **Main content:** Same structure as Songs 1 and 2 with the third song.
- **User action:** Listen, read, repeat, and mark complete.
- **Validation value:** High. Determines whether three songs feels like the right scope or too much/too little.

#### Three-day practice challenge

- **Purpose:** Encourage repetition and confidence. Extend the learning beyond a single reading.
- **Approximate length:** 1 page.
- **Main content:** A three-day plan with one small action per day. Day 1: listen and read. Day 2: practice pronunciation and record or self-check. Day 3: review, use one phrase in an original sentence, and answer the survey.
- **User action:** Commit to three short practice sessions.
- **Validation value:** Medium. Readers who complete the challenge are more likely to be high-intent learners.

#### Quick self-assessment

- **Purpose:** Give the learner a sense of progress and confidence.
- **Approximate length:** Half page.
- **Main content:** Three to five simple yes/no or confidence-check questions. For example: Can you pronounce one phrase from each song? Do you understand the natural meaning? Do you want to practice more?
- **User action:** Reflect on learning and feel capable.
- **Validation value:** Medium. Self-reported confidence is a learning signal.

#### Next steps and calls to action

- **Purpose:** Convert engaged readers into survey respondents and first-group candidates.
- **Approximate length:** 1 page.
- **Main content:** Primary CTA (survey), secondary CTA (first group), brand mention, and a brief description of what comes next in the product.
- **User action:** Click the survey link, the first-group link, or return to the channel.
- **Validation value:** Very high. CTA conversion is the strongest validation signal from the ebook.

#### Educational and copyright note

- **Purpose:** Legal clarity and editorial honesty.
- **Approximate length:** Half page or footer block.
- **Main content:** A clear statement that the guide uses short educational fragments, original pronunciation guidance, and natural translation. It does not reproduce full lyrics. It does not replace official lyrics, audio, or video platforms. Song title and artist attribution.
- **User action:** Read and trust the product.
- **Validation value:** Low direct. Required for legal-safe positioning.

## 7. Song-Selection Framework

### Important note

No final songs are selected in this branch. This framework defines the criteria and process for making the selection in `content/phase-0-ebook-song-selection`.

### Selection criteria

Listed in priority order. Criteria can be weighted or ranked during the selection process.

1. **Audience familiarity.** The learner should recognize or be curious about the song. Familiarity reduces resistance.
2. **Existing community interest.** Priority to songs mentioned in comments, requests, or survey responses from the English with Lyrics channel audience.
3. **Clear and useful pronunciation moments.** The song must contain short phrases with pronunciation features worth teaching: reductions, connected speech, tricky vowels, or silent letters.
4. **Moderate speed.** Avoid songs where the delivery is too fast for a beginner or intermediate learner to follow with guidance.
5. **Suitable vocabulary.** Words should be teachable and useful in real life. Avoid songs where the vocabulary is too specialized, obscure, or untranslatable.
6. **Practical phrases.** At least some phrases from the song should have real-life communicative value beyond the song context.
7. **Emotional or motivational connection.** The song should make the learner feel something. Emotional connection increases engagement and retention.
8. **Availability of an official audio or video source.** There must be an official YouTube video, Spotify track, or equivalent licensed source the reader can access for free.
9. **Suitability for beginner or intermediate learners.** The song should not require advanced English comprehension to engage with the short fragments chosen.
10. **Low dependence on slang.** Slang phrases are acceptable if they have clear natural translations, but songs that depend entirely on idiomatic or slang-heavy language are harder to teach at this level.
11. **No explicit or unsuitable central themes.** The central theme of the song should be appropriate for a general audience. Short phrases can be selected even from songs with mature themes if the selected fragments are themselves appropriate.
12. **Ability to analyze only short fragments.** The song must allow the creation of two to five genuinely short educational phrases without requiring sequential or near-complete lyric reproduction.
13. **Variety across the 3-song set.** The three songs should differ in tempo, mood, and category to test which type generates the most engagement.

### Provisional mix recommendation

- **One romantic or emotional song.** Slower tempo. Clear pronunciation. Emotional connection. Best for learners who want to understand feeling and expression in English.
- **One rock or pop classic.** Familiar and recognizable. High-frequency vocabulary. Rhythm and reductions. Best for adult learners who grew up with these songs.
- **One slower beginner-friendly song.** Accessible vocabulary. Short clear phrases. Lower tempo. Best for learners who feel intimidated by English music.

This mix tests three distinct audience segments simultaneously and provides evidence for which category should drive Phase 1 content.

### How survey and channel evidence should influence the decision

The ebook song selection must be informed by evidence, not assumptions. Before finalizing songs:

- Review survey responses for song requests and category preferences.
- Review which English with Lyrics channel videos have the highest engagement.
- Note which song categories appear in comments or direct audience feedback.
- If the survey shows a strong preference for one category, weight that category in the final selection.
- If channel evidence conflicts with survey evidence, prefer the survey because it captures active intent from an interested audience, not passive viewing behavior.

## 9. Per-Song Micro-Lesson Template

This template defines a reusable structure for each of the three selected songs. It should be applied consistently across all song sections in the final ebook draft.

### Template structure

**Song title and artist**
State the title and artist for attribution.

**Suggested learner level**
Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced. Keep to one or two levels.

**Why this song is useful**
One to three sentences explaining what makes this song a good educational choice. Focus on pronunciation, vocabulary, or emotional connection. Do not copy marketing language from artist pages.

**Link or QR code to official audio or video**
Provide the official YouTube link, Spotify link, or platform URL where the learner can listen for free. QR codes are optional and supplementary only; always include the text URL.

**Listen-first instruction**
Tell the learner to listen to the song once before reading the phrases. Set a specific, achievable goal: for example, "Escucha una vez y cuenta cuántas palabras puedes entender."

**Short educational phrases (3 per song as baseline; up to 5 if scope allows)**
Each phrase is presented in its own block with the following components:

- English phrase as it appears in the song (short fragment only, legally conservative).
- Escríbelo como suena pronunciation guide.
- Natural Spanish meaning.
- Key vocabulary (2–4 words maximum per phrase).
- Practical real-life use (an original sentence showing the phrase or vocabulary in a non-song context).

**One mini exercise**
A single low-friction activity. See Section 13 for approved exercise types. Never a full gap-fill or verse reconstruction.

**One speaking or repetition prompt**
A direct instruction to repeat the phrase aloud, record, or self-check. For example: "Repite esta frase tres veces con el audio oficial."

**One quick review**
A single question or reflection checking that the learner retained the key point of the section.

**Progress checkbox**
A small checkbox or equivalent marker so the learner can mark this song section complete.

### Legal and editorial reminders for this template

- Each excerpt must be short. Three to seven words is the target range per phrase block.
- The ebook must not reproduce complete verses, choruses, or lyrics in any combination that reconstructs the song.
- The lesson must add original explanation and practice. The fragment alone is not the lesson.
- The resource must not replace official lyric or music services.
- Final fragments require editorial and legal-safe review before publication. See Section 12 and the checklist in Section 19.

## 10. Escríbelo Como Suena Editorial Rules

This section summarizes the rules from `CONTENT_GUIDE.md` as applied specifically to the ebook context.

### Rules for learner-facing content

- **No IPA.** Do not use phonetic symbols such as /æ/, /θ/, or /ɪ/. They are unfamiliar and intimidating to most of the target audience.
- **Use Spanish-friendly pronunciation.** Write the English sounds using Spanish letters and combinations that approximate the English output. For example: "wil" for "will," "láv" for "love."
- **Use written accents only when they clarify stress.** Accents should signal which syllable to emphasize, not add grammatical marks. Use sparingly.
- **Prioritize readability over phonetic perfection.** A useful approximation that a reader can immediately say aloud is more valuable than a precise transcription they cannot decode.
- **Avoid misleading Spanish spellings.** If a Spanish spelling would lead the learner to a very different sound, revise it. For example, avoid combinations that create an unwanted hard /g/ or /x/ sound.
- **Maintain consistent conventions across all songs.** The same English sound must be written the same way throughout the entire ebook. Do not change spelling conventions between song sections.
- **Explain reductions or connected speech only when useful.** If a word is swallowed or merged in the song, note it briefly. Do not over-explain every phonological feature.
- **Keep the method approachable.** The tone should feel like advice from a patient bilingual friend, not a phonetics textbook.
- **Present it as a pronunciation bridge, not a scientific transcription.** Always frame Escríbelo como suena as an approximation that helps the learner get started. Never claim it is the only correct pronunciation.
- **Include a note that pronunciation can vary by singer, accent, and performance.** This is especially important for songs where the artist's accent or delivery style creates unusual sounds.

### Internal consistency checklist (pre-drafting)

Before drafting the song lessons, create a small reference table that documents:

- How to write the most common English vowel sounds in Escríbelo como suena for this ebook.
- How to write common consonant clusters (e.g., "th," "wh," "ng").
- How to mark primary stress.
- How to signal reduction or connected speech when relevant.

This table ensures all three song sections follow the same system. Review it before writing each lesson and update it only if a new sound pattern requires a new convention.

## 11. Translation and Vocabulary Rules

### Translation rules

- **Use natural Spanish, not rigid word-for-word translation.** A phrase that translates awkwardly word by word should be translated by its communicative meaning and feel.
- **Preserve the useful communicative meaning.** The learner needs to understand what the phrase means and when a native speaker would say it, not a literal dictionary equivalent.
- **Explain ambiguity only when it helps the learner.** If a phrase has two reasonable interpretations, mention it briefly. Do not turn it into a lengthy grammar note.
- **Keep vocabulary selections practical.** Prefer words that appear in everyday conversation over obscure or song-specific vocabulary.
- **Avoid overexplaining every word.** Not every word in a phrase needs a vocabulary note. Select 2–4 words per phrase that deliver the most educational value.
- **Prefer 2 to 4 useful vocabulary items per short phrase.** This keeps the vocabulary block manageable and scannable on mobile.
- **Include original example sentences rather than additional song lyrics.** Vocabulary examples must come from original sentences created for this ebook, not from other songs or third-party sources.
- **Clearly distinguish translation, explanation, and real-life usage.** Use consistent labels or block formatting so the reader always knows whether they are reading the Spanish meaning, a grammar or vocabulary note, or an original usage example.

### What counts as a vocabulary note in this ebook

A vocabulary note in the ebook covers:

- The English word or short phrase.
- Its natural Spanish meaning.
- One or two examples of when a native speaker would use it (outside the song).
- A note about register, level, or frequency if relevant (e.g., "Este phrasal verb es muy común en inglés informal").

Vocabulary notes must not reproduce additional song lines to illustrate a point.

## 12. Legal-Safe and Copyright Rules

This section aligns with `LEGAL_GUIDE.md` as applied specifically to this ebook.

### Prohibited content

- Full lyrics from any commercial song.
- Complete verses from any commercial song.
- Complete choruses from any commercial song.
- Extensive sequential fragments that together reconstruct a significant portion of a song.
- A layout designed to function as a lyric replacement or lyrics database.
- Translations copied from third-party lyrics sites or translation databases.
- Pronunciation transcriptions copied from external sources.
- Exercises copied or adapted from competitor worksheets.

### Required approach

- Use only short fragments necessary for educational explanation. The "short fragment" standard applies strictly: three to seven words per phrase block is the target. Longer fragments require explicit editorial and legal justification.
- Add original analysis, pronunciation guidance, vocabulary explanation, exercises, and practical examples around every fragment. The fragment exists to support the teaching, not the other way around.
- Link or direct users to official media (YouTube, Spotify, official artist platforms) instead of embedding unauthorized audio or reproducing audio content.
- Include source attribution for song title and artist in every song section and in the educational note.
- Include an educational-use and rights-reservation note in the footer or final page. Suggested text: "Esta guía usa fragmentos educativos cortos, pronunciación original, traducción natural y explicaciones transformativas. No publica letras completas ni reemplaza plataformas oficiales de música, video o lyrics. Títulos y artistas mencionados pertenecen a sus respectivos titulares."
- Flag uncertain fragments for internal review rather than expanding them. If a chosen phrase risks being too long or sequential, shorten it or select a different phrase.

### Important disclaimer

Educational intent does not automatically guarantee fair use or equivalent legal protection under Colombian, US, or international copyright law. The approach in this document is designed to be legally cautious, not legally certified. Before publishing paid content at scale or running many lessons based on commercial songs, consult a qualified attorney as noted in `LEGAL_GUIDE.md`.

## 13. Exercise Library

### Approved exercise types for this ebook

The following exercise types are approved for use in the ebook. Each must be completable in under two minutes and must not require the reproduction of lyrics beyond what is already shown in the lesson block.

- **Match the phrase with its natural meaning.** Show the short educational phrase and two or three meaning options in Spanish. The learner selects the most natural one.
- **Choose the most natural Spanish meaning.** A multiple-choice question with the phrase shown once and two to four Spanish translation options.
- **Mark the stressed syllable.** Show the Escríbelo como suena spelling and ask the learner to identify or confirm which syllable is stressed.
- **Repeat using the pronunciation guide.** A direct instruction to read the Escríbelo como suena text aloud, then listen to the official audio to compare.
- **Identify one useful vocabulary item.** Show the short phrase and ask the learner to write or say the word they would most like to remember.
- **Select when the phrase could be used in real life.** Present two or three everyday situations and ask which one fits the phrase's meaning.
- **Write a new original sentence using a vocabulary item.** Ask the learner to create one original sentence with one of the key vocabulary words. No song lyrics required.
- **Record yourself and self-evaluate.** Ask the learner to record their pronunciation on a phone and compare it to the official audio. Self-evaluation with no grade or external submission.
- **Compare first and second pronunciation attempts.** After practicing, ask the learner what changed between their first and second try.
- **Complete a very short original practice sentence.** Provide a sentence with one word missing (a vocabulary item from the lesson) and ask the learner to fill it in. The sentence must be original, not from the song.

### Prohibited exercise types

- Full-song gap-fill (requiring the learner to fill in an entire verse or chorus).
- Ordering complete lyrics in sequence.
- Reconstructing a verse from memory or partial prompts.
- Large transcription exercises where the learner writes out long song sections.
- Any exercise that requires the learner to read or engage with lyrics not already shown as a short educational excerpt in the lesson block.

## 14. Three-Day Practice Challenge

The challenge should appear after the three song sections. It is designed to encourage learners to return to the material and build confidence through repetition. Each day should require no more than 10–15 minutes of practice.

### Day 1: Listen and understand

- Play each official song once.
- While listening, try to identify one of the short phrases from the lesson.
- Read the natural Spanish meaning for each phrase after listening.
- Goal: connect the printed text to the real sound of the song.

### Day 2: Practice pronunciation

- Open the guide to the Escríbelo como suena section for each song.
- Repeat each phrase three times using the pronunciation guide.
- Record yourself saying one phrase from each song if possible.
- Compare your recording to the official audio.
- Goal: build confidence with the sound of the phrase, not perfection.

### Day 3: Review and act

- Review all selected phrases without looking at the pronunciation guide first.
- Complete the quick self-assessment in the guide.
- Use at least one learned phrase or vocabulary item in a new original sentence.
- Respond to the project survey or the first-group interest form.
- Goal: consolidate learning and convert engagement into a validation signal.

### Challenge design principles

- The challenge must be achievable during short breaks, commutes, or quiet moments. It is not a study session.
- No grading, no failure, no comparison to other learners.
- The final day must include the survey CTA organically as the "next step" after completing the practice.
- Frame it as progress and curiosity, not obligation.

## 16. Validation Signals and Metrics

### What the ebook can help measure

The ebook creates observable signals across four categories.

#### Interest signals

- Ebook request volume (Tally form submissions).
- Visits to `/ebook-gratis`.
- Delivery email open rate.
- Click rate from delivery email to `/ebook-gratis`.

#### Engagement signals

- Download clicks when the PDF exists.
- Survey click-through rate from the ebook or delivery page.
- Survey completion rate.
- First-group interest clicks.
- Preferred song category (from survey response, email replies, or form field).
- Replies or direct messages referencing the guide content.
- Completion self-report (if a survey question asks whether learners finished the guide).

#### High-intent signals

- Survey completion after downloading.
- First-group form submission.
- Direct replies requesting more lessons or asking about specific songs.
- Reported pronunciation or listening improvement.

#### Monetization signals (what the free ebook cannot validate alone)

The ebook does not validate willingness to pay. Monitoring these signals separately is important:

- Survey responses indicating price sensitivity and willingness to pay in COP.
- First-group form submissions where learners explicitly ask about pricing.
- Founder offer click-through rate if the ebook includes a mention of the first group.
- Direct messages or replies asking about pricing or enrollment.

These signals require follow-up forms, emails, or direct conversations. The ebook is the entry point, not the proof.

## 17. Design Handoff

This section documents requirements for the future PDF template. No design work is done in this branch. A separate branch (`design/phase-0-ebook-pdf-template`) will implement the visual template.

### Brand palette for the ebook

| Color | Hex | Recommended use |
|---|---|---|
| Black | `#010101` | Page background, header background, primary surface |
| Yellow | `#FEE296` | Ebook CTAs, warm highlights, vocabulary emphasis labels |
| Pink | `#FE9CE1` | Pronunciation highlights, primary CTAs, key phrase blocks |
| Purple | `#CC9DEA` | Secondary accents, progress markers, borders, learning cards |

### Logo assets

Use official logo files from `public/brand/`. For a dark-background PDF: `logo-claro.svg` or `logo-fondo-transparente-B.png`. Do not recolor, stretch, crop, or redraw logo files. Follow all rules in `docs/design/BRAND_ASSETS.md`.

### Layout recommendations

- **Format:** Mobile-first. Recommended: A5 (148 × 210 mm) or equivalent vertical format. This remains readable on a phone without zooming.
- **Page count:** 8 to 14 pages including cover and final note.
- **Typography:** Strong hierarchy with at least three levels — title/heading, body, and label/caption. Accessible font sizes (minimum 14px equivalent for body text in PDF).
- **Paragraphs:** Short. No paragraph should exceed three to four sentences.
- **Cards and practice blocks:** Use cards or boxed blocks to visually separate phrase blocks from explanatory text.
- **Pronunciation highlighting:** Consistent visual treatment for Escríbelo como suena text. For example: a pink-tinted block or yellow label.
- **Contrast:** High contrast throughout. White or cream body text on dark backgrounds. Black text on yellow CTAs.
- **Spacing:** Generous whitespace between sections. The guide should feel open, not packed.
- **Links:** All CTAs must be clickable links in the PDF export. Test on both desktop and mobile PDF viewers.
- **QR codes:** Optional and supplementary only. Always include the text URL alongside any QR code. Do not use QR codes as the only access method.
- **Decorative elements:** Minimal. Music-inspired accents (lines, dots, icons) are acceptable if they do not reduce readability or introduce non-brand colors.

## 18. Content Production Workflow

This workflow applies to the future `content/phase-0-ebook-draft` branch. It is documented here so the production team can plan the full sequence before starting.

1. **Review survey and channel evidence.** Confirm audience familiarity, preferred categories, and song requests before selecting songs.

5. **Draft natural Spanish meanings.** Follow the translation rules in Section 11.

7. **Run editorial consistency review.** Check that pronunciation conventions are consistent, vocabulary notes are appropriately scoped, and the voice is consistent across all three song sections.
