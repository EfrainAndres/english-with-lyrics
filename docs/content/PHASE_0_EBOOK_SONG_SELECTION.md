# Phase 0 Ebook Song Selection

## Overview

This document selects the provisional three-song set for the first Sing Pronounce Repeat / English with Lyrics free ebook and records the full selection process, scoring rationale, evidence gaps, backup songs, and conditions that could change the selection before PDF publication.

All scores in this document are editorial estimates based on pedagogical, brand, and project evidence available in the repository. No audience analytics, streaming data, or survey results have been collected yet. Audience-evidence scores are deliberately conservative to reflect this gap.

This phase does not select lyric fragments, draft lesson content, or reproduce any song text. Fragment selection is reserved for `content/phase-0-ebook-draft`.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document records:

- The constraints and criteria driving the selection.
- An individual weighted evaluation of each candidate song.
- A set-level comparison of at least three possible three-song combinations.
- The provisional final recommendation.
- Backup songs and the conditions under which they replace a selected song.
- An evidence gap register identifying what is still missing and what decisions it could change.
- A song approval checklist that each selected song must pass before drafting begins.
- A decision log separating confirmed choices from open questions.

### What this document does not do

- Select or reproduce lyric fragments.
- Claim that audience familiarity or commercial appeal has been verified externally.
- Guarantee legal clearance for any song.
- Make the selection permanent. The provisional set may change based on survey responses, channel analytics, or editorial review during drafting.

## 2. Selection Constraints

The following constraints are confirmed and must apply to the final selection regardless of how scores or survey evidence evolve.

**Format constraints**

- Exactly 3 songs for the first ebook. Expanding to 4 or 5 songs is a Phase 1 decision.
- One romantic or emotional song.
- One rock or pop classic.
- One slow or beginner-friendly song.
- Songs may serve more than one category, but the full set must feel varied in mood, tempo, and genre.

**Content constraints**

- Avoid making all three songs emotionally or musically too similar. If Songs 1 and 2 are both slow romantic ballads, Song 3 must provide meaningful contrast.
- Avoid songs dominated by very fast singing, heavy slang, or explicit central themes.
- Each song must support short, independently useful, educational fragments. A song that is otherwise excellent but does not contain teachable short phrases at a beginner or intermediate level is unsuitable for this ebook.

**Legal and editorial constraints**

- No final phrases are selected or reproduced in this document.
- No full lyrics appear in this document.
- Official audio or video must be available through a legitimate licensed source (YouTube official channel, Spotify, Apple Music, or equivalent). This will be confirmed during drafting.
- Final fragment selection requires separate editorial and legal-safe review before the ebook is published. See Section 12 of `docs/content/PHASE_0_EBOOK_CONTENT_PLAN.md`.

## 3. Weighted Evaluation Model

### Scoring weights

Criterion weights total 100 points. Each criterion is scored 1–5 and the weighted contribution is calculated as: `(score ÷ 5) × weight`.

| Criterion | Weight | Max weighted points |
|---|---|---|
| Pronunciation clarity | 20 | 20 |
| Beginner/intermediate accessibility | 15 | 15 |
| Useful real-life language potential | 15 | 15 |
| Listening-practice value | 10 | 10 |
| Audience familiarity or documented community interest | 10 | 10 |
| Emotional or motivational connection | 10 | 10 |
| Variety contribution to the 3-song set | 10 | 10 |
| Legal/editorial suitability for short-fragment analysis | 10 | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** | **100** |

### Score scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Weak — significant limitation for this criterion |
| 2 | Limited — below average; requires specific justification to include |
| 3 | Acceptable — meets the minimum standard for this criterion |
| 4 | Strong — clearly suits this criterion without major caveats |
| 5 | Excellent — outstanding for this criterion relative to the candidate pool |

### Score calculation example

A song scoring 4 on Pronunciation Clarity (weight 20) earns `(4 ÷ 5) × 20 = 16` weighted points from that criterion. Scores across all eight criteria are summed to produce the weighted total out of 100.

### Transparency note

Scores are editorial estimates. They reflect the project team's reading of each song's educational properties based on general musical knowledge and the pedagogical framework in `docs/content/PHASE_0_EBOOK_CONTENT_PLAN.md`. They are not objective measurements. Audience-evidence scores are treated conservatively throughout because no survey or channel analytics data has been collected. These estimates should be revised once evidence is available.

## 4. Candidate Evaluation

No lyrics, quoted phrases, or song fragments appear in this section.

### Score rationale notes

**Pronunciation clarity** reflects how clearly words are enunciated, how useful the singing delivery is for a learner practicing an approximation, and how accessible the sounds are to Spanish speakers. Songs with heavy distortion, extremely fast delivery, or non-standard vocal registers score lower.

**Accessibility** reflects how easily a beginner or intermediate Spanish-speaking learner can engage with selected short fragments. Songs with very fast verses, extended instrumental sections, or complex vocabulary score lower.

**Practical language potential** reflects whether the song contains short phrases that carry real communicative value outside the song. Songs with highly poetic or idiomatic phrases that do not transfer to everyday conversation score lower.

**Listening-practice value** reflects how well a learner can use the song to practice active listening at a manageable pace. Minimal accompaniment, deliberate delivery, and clear separation of syllables increase this score.

**Audience familiarity** scores are conservative throughout because no survey results, channel analytics, or audience requests have been collected for this project yet. Songs with known cross-generational presence in Latin American popular culture receive a slight advantage. No score above 4 is assigned in this criterion without documented evidence.

**Emotional connection** reflects the motivational pull of the song. Higher emotional resonance increases the chance that learners stay engaged with the lesson.

**Variety contribution** is evaluated relative to the full 3-song set, not in isolation. A romantic ballad that scores 5 on variety when it is the only romantic song may score lower if another romantic ballad is also selected.

**Legal/editorial suitability** reflects how easily the song can yield three to five short, independently educational fragments without sequential reconstruction of a verse or chorus.

## 5. Set-Level Evaluation

Individual scores identify the strongest candidates, but the three-song set must also work as a coherent learning journey. This section compares three viable combinations.

Criteria for set-level evaluation:

- Musical and genre variety.
- Learner-level balance within the set.
- Voice and gender variety where pedagogically useful.
- Tempo variety (the set should include at least one clearly slower and one more rhythmically dynamic song).
- Emotional variety (the set should not be emotionally monotonous).
- Category coverage (romantic, rock/pop classic, and beginner-friendly/practical).
- Risk that two songs teach very similar pronunciation patterns or vocabulary.
- Overall brand fit for Sing Pronounce Repeat.
- Ability to keep the ebook short, coherent, and completable in one sitting.

---

### Set A: A Thousand Years + Nothing Else Matters + The Reason

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Songs | A Thousand Years (Christina Perri), Nothing Else Matters (Metallica), The Reason (Hoobastank) |
| Category coverage | Romantic ballad + Rock classic + Pop/Rock with practical vocabulary |
| Difficulty range | Beginner → Intermediate → Intermediate |
| Tempo variety | Very slow → Mid-tempo rock → Moderate pop/rock |
| Voice variety | Female (solo piano) → Male (rock) → Male (pop/rock) |
| Emotional variety | Romantic devotion → Raw intensity → Regret and growth |

**Main strength:** Clear three-genre separation. Nothing Else Matters provides the most unmistakable "rock classic" identity of any candidate. The Reason has strong practical vocabulary and clear American English pronunciation. A Thousand Years anchors the beginner slot with outstanding clarity.

**Main weakness:** Nothing Else Matters is the most difficult song in the candidate pool from an accessibility standpoint. Some of its vocal delivery is less clear in certain sections, which creates challenges for Escríbelo como suena guidance. The audience evidence for all three remains unverified, though all three are well-known outside the repository.

**Intended learner journey:** The reader begins with a familiar, slow, emotionally warm song. They then encounter a more challenging rock classic that tests their listening confidence. They finish with a pop/rock song that bridges both styles and reinforces practical English phrases.

**Recommendation level:** Strong alternative. Best choice if survey evidence confirms significant rock-category demand and learner confidence is higher than estimated.

**Main strength:** Still Loving You is explicitly noted in `docs/content/PHASE_0_EBOOK_CONTENT_PLAN.md` as "known in Latin American markets," which gives it the highest justified audience evidence score of any rock candidate. **Intended learner journey:** The reader begins with a gentle, slow ballad. They advance to a rock ballad with higher emotional intensity and a more complex vocal delivery. They conclude with a pop/rock song where vocabulary and pronunciation are directly applicable to everyday English.

**Recommendation level:** Provisional recommendation. Best fit for the Phase 0 audience given the Latin American market recognition of Still Loving You and the practical language strength of The Reason. See Section 6.

The set prioritizes practical learning outcomes over musical excitement.

**Recommendation level:** Strong alternative. Best choice if survey evidence confirms that learners prioritize conversational English over musical variety, or if Thank You's audience familiarity proves higher than estimated.

## 7. Backup Songs

### Primary backup: Nothing Else Matters — Metallica

**Replaces:** Still Loving You (Song 2, rock slot)

**Replacement conditions:**

- Survey evidence shows that learners strongly prefer harder or more iconic rock classics over ballad-style rock.
- Still Loving You's fragments prove unsuitable during drafting due to accent, speed, or sequential reconstruction risk.
- The three-song set feels emotionally too similar (both Still Loving You and A Thousand Years are romantic ballads) and a harder contrast is editorially justified.
- Community evidence from the English with Lyrics channel shows higher interest in Metallica than in Scorpions.

**Why Nothing Else Matters is a strong backup:** It is one of the most recognizable rock songs globally and fills the rock classic slot with unambiguous genre identity. The verses contain deliberate, clear sections that are usable for short-fragment teaching. Its emotional intensity provides strong contrast with A Thousand Years.

**Remaining concern if activated:** Nothing Else Matters is more difficult for beginners. If it replaces Still Loving You, the ebook's learner-level range shifts upward and the beginner-friendly balance decreases. This should be noted in the ebook's "how to use" section if this backup is activated.

---

### Secondary backup: Thank You — Dido

**Replaces:** The Reason (Song 3, practical-language slot)

**Replacement conditions:**

- Survey evidence shows that learners want conversational, practical English above all other goals.
- The Reason's fragments prove too song-specific or idiomatic during drafting to transfer reliably to real-life use.
- Channel evidence shows higher engagement with Dido's content than with Hoobastank's.
- The three-song set needs a quieter, more conversational third song to prevent tonal overload.

**Why Thank You is a strong backup:** It scores highest on practical language potential of all eight candidates. Its conversational delivery style is excellent for teaching connected speech, natural reductions, and everyday English in a low-pressure context. The gentle tone provides emotional relief after the intensity of Songs 1 and 2.

**Remaining concern if activated:** Thank You's audience familiarity in the target demographic is the most uncertain of all candidates. If it replaces The Reason, the ebook's audience recognition strength decreases. A brief song-context section in the lesson will need to orient learners who do not know the song before teaching begins.

## 9. Song Approval Checklist

Each selected song must pass this checklist before the drafting phase begins in `content/phase-0-ebook-draft`. This checklist may be run informally before drafting or formally documented in that branch.

### Educational checklist

- [ ] The song is suitable for beginner to intermediate Spanish-speaking learners given its tempo, vocabulary, and delivery.
- [ ] The song contains useful pronunciation moments that can be taught using Escríbelo como suena without IPA.
- [ ] The vocabulary in at least two to three short phrases has practical real-life communicative value.
- [ ] The song complements the other two selected songs without duplicating their primary learning objective.
- [ ] A short guided lesson using this song can fit within two to three pages at the ebook's target scope of 8–14 total pages.

### Editorial checklist

- [ ] Song title and artist have been verified as correct.
- [ ] Approximate learner level has been documented for this specific song.
- [ ] The song's learning role within the ebook (romantic/beginner anchor, rock contrast, practical vocabulary) has been documented.
- [ ] No two of the three selected songs share the same primary learning objective in a way that would make one redundant.

### Legal-safe checklist

- [ ] No full lyrics will be needed for this song's lesson.
- [ ] No complete verse or chorus will be needed.
- [ ] Three to five short educational fragments can be selected that are independently useful without sequential reconstruction.
- [ ] Fragment selection will remain non-sequential (fragments do not run consecutively through the song).
- [ ] Original Escríbelo como suena guidance, natural translation, vocabulary notes, and exercises will add transformation around every fragment.
- [ ] An official licensed audio or video source (YouTube official channel, Spotify, or equivalent) can be linked rather than embedded or downloaded.

### Evidence checklist

- [ ] All known evidence supporting the song's selection has been documented.
- [ ] All assumptions made in the absence of evidence are clearly labeled as assumptions.
- [ ] Missing evidence relevant to the selection has been recorded in the evidence gap register.
- [ ] Replacement conditions are documented so the selection can be changed before publication if evidence contradicts it.
