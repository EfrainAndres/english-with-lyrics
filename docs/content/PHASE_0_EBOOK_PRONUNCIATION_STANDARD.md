## 1. Purpose and scope

This document defines one consistent `Escríbelo como suena` standard for the approved 32-page free ebook so that all nine practice pages use the same notation, the same difficult-sound guidance and the same learner caveats.

Scope:

- Applies to the nine already approved short English fragments and their Spanish-friendly pronunciation bridges.
- Governs notation, stress marks, temporary-support notation and explanatory notes only.
- Does **not** change, expand, shorten or reorder any approved English fragment.
- Does **not** add adjacent song lines, reconstruct a verse or chorus, or introduce new commercial-song text.
- Is a complete draft. Qualified pronunciation review is pending.

## 2. What `Escríbelo como suena` is

`Escríbelo como suena` is the Sing Pronunce Repeat way of writing English pronunciation using letters a Spanish speaker can already read out loud. It is a practical starting point that lowers the barrier to saying an English phrase for the first time, before the learner listens to the official audio and adjusts.

- It is a bridge toward real audio.
- It is designed for Spanish-speaking beginner and intermediate learners.
- It uses letters and sounds the learner already knows from Spanish.

## 3. What it is not

- It is **not** the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) and uses no IPA symbols in learner-facing copy.
- It is **not** an exact or perfect pronunciation.
- It does **not** replace official audio; official or authorized audio is always the final listening reference.
- It does **not** promise that the learner will sound identical to the original singer.

## 4. Global notation conventions

One consistent system across all nine practices:

- Write every bridge in **lowercase**.
- Use a **written accent mark** to indicate the strongest syllable of each content word (for example `clóu·zer`, `bi·fór`).
- Separate **words** with `·` surrounded by spaces: `word · word`.
- Separate **syllables inside a single word** with `·` and no surrounding spaces: `pér·fect`.
- Treat vowel glides such as `ai`, `éi`, `óu` and `iú` as **one continuous movement**, never as two separate Spanish vowels.
- Normalize the English `/w/` onset to a soft Spanish **`u` glide** (for example `uíl`, `uéi`), not the letter `w` and not a hard Spanish `v`.
- Do **not** use IPA in learner-facing copy.
- Never claim the bridge is an exact pronunciation.
- Always treat official audio as the final listening reference.
- Make differences caused by **singer, accent, speed and musical style** explicit wherever they affect a fragment.

## 5. Difficult-sound conventions

Conservative, reusable guidance to be applied consistently:

- **English soft-air `h`** (as in *heart* → `jart`): present it as a soft breath of air. Never present it as the strong guttural Spanish `j`.
- **Voiced `th`** (as in *there* → `dér`): a temporary Spanish-friendly bridge written with `d`, plus guidance to rest the tongue lightly near the teeth and let the air out **with voice**.
- **Voiceless `th`** (as in *through* → `zrú`): a separate temporary bridge written with `z`, plus guidance to place the tongue lightly between the teeth and let the air out **without voice**. The `z`/`s` spelling is only an approximation.
- **English `r`**: approximate it softly. Do **not** instruct learners to trill or roll it like a Spanish `rr`.
- **Final consonants** such as `t`, `d`, `s` and `z`: preserve their importance. Flag when dropping the final consonant changes the meaning (for example the final `t` in *can't* versus *can*).
- **English vowel contrasts** that Spanish does not reproduce exactly (for example the open vowel in *all* → `ól`): describe them in plain words rather than IPA.
- **Initial `st` and similar clusters**: any added Spanish support vowel appears as **temporary**, shown in parentheses, with an explicit reduction goal — for example `(e)stép`. The parentheses signal that the learner should lean on the support at first and reduce it over time.
- **Connected speech**: describe it only where it directly helps the approved fragment (for example the informal contraction `I'll be`).
- **Singer accent**: distinguish neutral learning guidance from the actual performance so learners are not misled when the recording differs from the written bridge.

The normalized bridges may change punctuation, stress marks, temporary-support notation and explanatory notes. They must not change or expand the nine approved English fragments.

## 7. Learner-facing caveats

Every practice page should reinforce, in plain Spanish:

- This is a practical bridge, not IPA and not an exact pronunciation.
- Temporary support sounds (shown in parentheses) should be reduced over time.
- The official audio is the final reference; listen and adjust.
- Pronunciation can change with the singer, the accent, the speed and the musical style.
- The goal is to understand more and gain confidence, not to sound identical to the original.
